A PRD reads as a decision document, not a feature list. The opening sentence states the problem in business terms and the cost of doing nothing.
Scope is the set of behaviors that must change for the feature to be considered shipped; out of scope is the set of behaviors we are explicitly choosing not to alter, with reasoning.
Success metrics are concrete, measurable, and tied to the originating problem. Vanity counts (clicks, impressions) belong in the body of the doc only when justified.
Rollout is staged: internal dogfood first, then a small percentage of admin accounts gated by feature flag, then progressive expansion with explicit fail-fast criteria.
Risks include adoption risk (admins who refuse to enroll), recovery risk (lockout becomes the new failure mode), and security risk (a poorly chosen recovery path negates the entire feature).
SOC2 findings are addressed in the success metrics, not in marketing copy. The PRD names the auditor finding and the verification path the auditor will accept.
The PRD calls out non-obvious decisions: TOTP vs WebAuthn, recovery codes vs admin-assisted reset, mandatory vs opt-in for the first wave, and the deadline by which mandatory enrollment becomes effective.
Out of scope is treated as a first-class section: mobile, customer (non-admin) accounts, third-party SSO integrations, and SCIM-managed enrollment are deferred with reasoning.
The risk section enumerates the rollback path: how to disable the feature flag, how to handle accounts mid-enrollment, and the on-call playbook for "all admins locked out" scenarios.
