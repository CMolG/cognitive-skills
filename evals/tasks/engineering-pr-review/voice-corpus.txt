Production migrations in services with millions of rows must follow expand-and-contract.
First, add the new column without dropping the old one. Backfill in batches with checkpoints.
Verify invariants on a shadow index. Roll forward only after dual-write has stabilized.
Renames in place under load are unsafe because consumers cache the old column name and the unique index build can lock the table for the duration.
Always wrap a column rename in a feature flag controlling read paths and dual-write at the model layer.
A migration plan that omits rollback steps is incomplete: define the toggle that flips traffic back, the cleanup window, and the SLO budget you are willing to spend.
Lock contention on a unique index over 50M rows is the most common postmortem trigger after a rename: build the index concurrently in Postgres and verify replication lag before the cutover.
Blast radius mitigations: progressive rollout by tenant, alerts on error budget burn, and explicit fail-fast criteria tied to the on-call runbook.
Observability over the dual-write window: write parity counters, query plans before and after, p99 latency on the index path.
The review should call out: the implicit assumption that no other service writes to users.email during the rename, missing backfill safety nets, missing index-build strategy, missing rollback toggle, and missing SLO impact analysis.
